## LinkedIn Page post copy

### November 2 — Small home mandir

A home mandir can feel peaceful without taking over the room. Start with the practical questions: where will daily पूजा items go, can the space be cleaned easily, is the light comfortable in the evening, and does the arrangement leave a clear route through the home?

For a compact setup, a defined niche, a small drawer or concealed shelf, and warm, well-shielded lighting can do more than adding lots of decoration. The right answer depends on the room and the family's routine.

We put together a simple guide for planning a small home mandir in Mohali and Tricity homes: https://www.dmsstudio.in/blog/small-home-mandir-design-mohali/

What matters most in your pooja space: storage, lighting, or a quiet location?

### November 9 — Winter comfort

When a room feels cold in winter, changing the furniture is rarely the first thing to try. Look at the layers already in the room: daylight, evening lighting, window comfort, rugs, curtains and the way seating is arranged.

Use more than one light source so the room can shift from daytime tasks to a softer evening. Add textiles where they help comfort and acoustics, while keeping walkways and frequently used surfaces easy to manage.

Our new guide shares practical winter home ideas for Mohali and the Tricity: https://www.dmsstudio.in/blog/warm-winter-home-interiors-mohali/

Which small change makes your home feel cosier without a full makeover?

### November 16 — Guest bedroom

A guest bedroom shouldn't have to sit unused for most of the year. Before choosing a bed or a colour palette, decide what else the room needs to do: occasional work, storage, reading, or a comfortable place for a family member.

The useful details are often simple: a clear route around the bed, reachable charging, a bedside surface, flexible storage and lighting that works for more than one activity. A coherent palette can make the room feel considered without making it feel formal.

We have a room-planning guide for guest bedrooms in Indian homes: https://www.dmsstudio.in/blog/guest-bedroom-design-indian-homes/

How does your guest room work when no one is visiting?

### November 23 — Modular kitchen

Choosing a modular kitchen finish is exciting, but the layout should come first. Map the actual cooking routine, appliance sizes, service points, storage needs and the path between sink, preparation area and hob. Then compare shutters, counters and hardware against that plan.

This order helps catch awkward clearances and missing storage before fabrication begins. It also makes it easier to compare proposals on what is included, not only on a finish photo.

Our checklist covers the planning questions to ask before selecting kitchen finishes: https://www.dmsstudio.in/blog/modular-kitchen-planning-mohali-checklist/

What would you plan first: workflow, storage, or appliance locations?

### November 30 — Christmas in a small living room

Festive styling works best when the room still works for everyday life. In a compact living room, choose one clear focal point, keep the main walking route open, and use a few repeated colours or materials rather than decorating every surface.

Before adding a tree or extra seating, check door swings, access to the balcony and the route between the sofa and dining area. Warm lamps and a small cluster of ornaments can make the room feel seasonal without crowding it.

More ideas for a festive, usable small living room: https://www.dmsstudio.in/blog/christmas-decor-small-indian-living-room/

Do you prefer one statement decoration or smaller touches around the room?

### December 7 — Office fit-out

An office fit-out is easier to coordinate when the design brief comes before the contractor schedule. Start with how people move through the space, where calls and focused work happen, what needs privacy, and where power, data and storage are required.

Once those needs are clear, the team can coordinate lighting, furniture, acoustic treatment and services around the same plan. Early coordination can reduce avoidable changes during execution.

Our Mohali office-planning guide covers the questions to settle before a 2027 fit-out: https://www.dmsstudio.in/blog/office-interior-planning-mohali-2027/

What is the one thing a new office must support well: focus, collaboration, or client meetings?

### December 14 — AI images and real planning

AI room images can be useful for exploring mood, colours and broad design directions. They cannot verify a site's measurements, existing plumbing, electrical points, daylight through the day, or whether a piece of furniture fits through the door.

Use an image as a conversation starter, then test the idea against the real room and the people who use it. That is where a beautiful reference becomes a workable plan.

We explain the difference in our guide to AI visuals and real home planning: https://www.dmsstudio.in/blog/ai-interior-design-vs-real-home-planning/

Which part of a design decision do you find hardest to visualise before work begins?

### December 21 — Comparing proposals

When comparing interior proposals for a 3BHK home, the total alone rarely tells the whole story. Check whether the scope lists room-by-room work, material specifications, hardware, site measurements, electrical and lighting work, installation, exclusions, and how changes are handled.

Ask each team to clarify the same items in writing. A clear scope makes it easier to compare like with like and spot assumptions before work starts.

Use our checklist before comparing 3BHK interior proposals in Mohali: https://www.dmsstudio.in/blog/3bhk-interior-quotation-checklist-mohali/

What detail do you always ask a designer to explain?

### December 28 — Planning for 2027

Planning a home interior in 2027? Start with the decisions that affect everything else: a measured plan, priorities for each room, what you want to keep, a realistic sequence, and the people who need to approve key choices.

Layouts and services should be settled before detailed finishes and procurement. Leaving time for site checks and revisions can make the process calmer, especially when custom work is involved.

### November 5 — Planning a small pooja corner

For a small home mandir, I would plan the daily routine before the look: where पूजा essentials go, how the niche gets cleaned, whether the diya is safely positioned, and how the light feels at night. A shallow drawer or closed shelf often keeps a compact setup calmer than adding more shelves. Also check that the mandir doesn't narrow a passage or interfere with a door swing. I'm sharing this from my work at a Mohali interior-design studio; every home's site and family routine are different.

### November 19 — A guest room that earns its keep

One question that helps with a guest room: what should it do on the 300-plus days when nobody is staying over? A desk, reading chair or well-planned storage can make the room useful year-round, but keep the bed route and bedside surface comfortable for visitors. If the room is compact, test the furniture footprint with tape before buying. I'm an interior designer with DMS Studio in Mohali; happy to compare ideas, but the right layout depends on the actual dimensions.

### December 3 — Festive styling in a small room

For a small living room during the holidays, I start by protecting the everyday route: entrance to sofa, sofa to dining, and access to windows or balcony. Then I choose one focal point and repeat just a couple of colours or materials. That usually feels more composed than adding small decorations to every surface. Keep candles away from curtains and greenery, and don't overload sockets with decorative lighting. I'm with DMS Studio, an interior-design studio in Mohali; this is general planning advice, not a product recommendation.

### December 24 — Comparing interior proposals

When comparing interior-design proposals, ask each team to describe the same scope: room-by-room work, material make and finish, hardware, electrical/lighting changes, installation, exclusions and how revisions are treated. If a line is vague, ask for the exact specification in writing before comparing totals. A short scope checklist can save confusion later. I work with DMS Studio in Mohali, so I'm sharing this with that affiliation disclosed; always judge a proposal against your own site and requirements.
