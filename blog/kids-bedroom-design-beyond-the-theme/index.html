Room by room

Kids’ bedroom design: make a football theme work beyond the mural

A themed children’s room can feel personal without making every surface permanent. Plan the practical layout first, then use colour, graphics and accessories to express the child’s interest.

By DMS StudioPublished 2026-10-15Updated 2026-10-15

Editorial photograph · Unsplash. Not a DMS project.

Quick answer

Keep a child’s bedroom adaptable by making storage, study lighting, circulation and bed placement the long-term foundation. Use replaceable elements—art, bedding, accessories and a focused feature wall—to express a football theme.

Begin with sleep, study and movement

List the activities the room must support: sleeping, dressing, reading, homework, play and storing sports equipment. Measure the bed, desk and wardrobe, then check clear routes between them. Keep frequently used items accessible and avoid placing storage where it blocks doors, windows or natural light. If the room is shared, identify each child’s storage and study needs separately before combining the layout.

Choose which elements carry the theme

A football theme can come through a mural, a graphic, bedding, framed prints, colour accents or a display for a favourite ball or jersey. Choose one main feature and a small number of supporting details so the room still feels restful. Permanent joinery can stay simple and useful; softer items are easier to change as a child’s interests evolve. Discuss the scale and position of graphics with the child so the feature feels intentional rather than crowded.

Make storage easy to use

Think about what needs a home: school books, clothes, toys, shoes, sports gear and seasonal items. Low shelves and labelled drawers can make everyday tidying more approachable. Use closed storage for items that tend to make the room feel visually busy, while keeping a few meaningful objects visible. The internal layout of a wardrobe matters as much as its exterior: hanging, folded clothes and drawers need different arrangements.

Give study and bedtime separate light

A desk benefits from focused light positioned to reduce shadows and screen glare. General room lighting supports movement and getting dressed; a softer bedside source can make reading more comfortable. Plan switches and sockets with the furniture position in mind, and have electrical requirements checked by a qualified professional. Curtains or blinds should also be considered alongside daylight and sleep routines.

Keep the room adaptable

Children grow, and their storage, study and display needs change. Leave space for a larger desk or different furniture where the plan allows. Keep the foundation—bed access, storage and safe circulation—useful even if a mural or colour palette changes. Choose finishes that can be maintained in daily use and avoid treating a theme as a fixed identity the child must keep for years.

A DMS project example: Evaan Joshi’s bedroom

The Evaan Joshi case study documents a children’s bedroom designed around a young football fan. Its photographs show a football mural, blue-and-white palette, themed bedding, study desk and coordinated storage. These project details are visible in the case study; other rooms will have different dimensions and family needs. Use the example to discuss how a child’s interest can guide colour and imagery while the room still serves sleep, study and storage.

How can a football bedroom stay stylish as a child grows?

Keep permanent furniture and storage practical, and use changeable items such as bedding, prints and accessories to express the theme. A single feature wall can make the room feel personal without making every future update expensive.

Useful answers

Common questions

Should a football mural cover every wall?+

Usually a focused wall or graphic gives the theme a clear place without overwhelming the room. Choose its position after planning the bed, desk and main sightlines.

How can I plan storage for a child’s room?+

List clothes, school items, toys and sports equipment separately, then assign accessible storage to everyday items and higher or closed storage to occasional-use belongings.

Can the room be designed for a child’s future needs?+

Yes. Keep the core layout flexible, allow for changing study needs where possible, and use replaceable decor for interests that may change.
