Planning an Office Interior in Mohali for 2027: A Practical Brief

Before a new office fit-out begins, define the team’s routines, visitor journey, technology needs and project responsibilities. This practical brief helps Mohali businesses prepare for a site-based design discussion.

By DMS StudioPublished 2026-12-07Practical design guidance
Quick answer

Before planning an office interior, list the headcount, work patterns, visitor needs, meeting requirements, storage, equipment and target move date. Measure the site and confirm building, landlord and service constraints before design is finalised. Coordinate furniture, lighting, power, data, acoustics and accessibility with the relevant professionals. Allow time for approvals, procurement and installation; the programme depends on the property and agreed scope.

Map a working day, not just a floor plan

List the activities that happen in the office: focused work, calls, collaboration, client meetings, reception, printing and storage. Notice how people and visitors move between them. A useful layout supports the team’s actual routine and keeps busy routes from cutting through quiet work areas. Include changes you expect in the next year, such as additional seats or more hybrid meetings, without overbuilding for an uncertain headcount.

Check the site and the building conditions early

Confirm dimensions, columns, windows, existing services, entry points, landlord requirements and any building approvals before committing to a layout. Identify what can stay and what needs to change. A site survey is essential before detailed drawings; photographs alone cannot confirm concealed services or exact dimensions. Coordinate fire, electrical, HVAC and other technical requirements with the appropriate qualified professionals.

Plan power, data, light and sound together

Workstations, meeting rooms and reception each need different combinations of light and connectivity. List screens, printers, routers, video-call equipment and charging needs. Consider how daylight and glare affect screens. Acoustic privacy may depend on partitions, doors, ceiling finishes and room placement, so address it while the layout is still flexible. Have technical installations reviewed by the relevant consultants and trades.

Set a clear scope and move-in sequence

Separate design decisions from procurement and site work. Confirm who measures, approves drawings, orders furniture, coordinates trades and signs off completed work. Identify dependencies such as landlord access, service shutdowns, delivery routes and workstation installation. Build the schedule around those facts and allow for decisions and material lead times. Do not promise a move-in date until the scope and site constraints are checked.

Make the workplace reflect the business

A considered reception, meeting room and shared area can express a company’s character, but the visual concept should support everyday work. Select a few durable finishes and repeat them consistently. What to bring to the first design discussion

Bring a plan if available, current site photographs, expected occupancy, business routines, equipment list, landlord guidelines and a target timeline. Note what must remain operational during the work. These details help the designer identify questions that need resolving before the fit-out scope can be priced and scheduled.

Useful answers

Common questions

What should I plan first for a new office?+

Start with the team’s work patterns, expected headcount, meeting needs, visitor journey, equipment and building constraints. Then confirm the site dimensions and service conditions.

How long does an office interior project take?+

Timing varies with site condition, approvals, scope, decisions, procurement and installation. A realistic programme can be discussed after those details are reviewed.

Can an office fit-out happen while the business is operating?+

Sometimes a phased approach is possible, but it depends on access, safety, noise, services and the type of work. Plan continuity with the building team and contractors.
