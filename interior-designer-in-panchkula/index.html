Planning a home for shared and private routines

Give shared rooms more than one purpose

A living room may need to host visitors, family evenings and occasional work. Discuss seating, circulation and accessible power together. A furniture plan can reveal conflicts before you invest in fixed joinery or a large dining table.

Keep private rooms genuinely personal

Bedroom layouts should reflect who uses them. Consider bedside lighting, wardrobe access, study areas and the space needed to move comfortably. Requirements for children, guests or older family members belong in the brief before the style discussion.

Use daylight as a design input

Visit rooms at different times and photograph how light reaches the surfaces. Review finish samples in the intended room wherever possible. Curtains, reflective surfaces and task lighting should respond to that specific space rather than a generic moodboard.
